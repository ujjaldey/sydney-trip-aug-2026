Grand Pacific Drive is one of Australia's most scenic coastal routes — 140 km of cliff-hugging roads from Royal National Park down to Nowra. In September (early spring), the coast is green after winter rains, skies are clear, and the southern right whale migration overlaps with humpback season. We're focusing on **less-crowded natural beaches** — skipping the tourist traps and going where locals go.

## Day 1 — Friday

> **Drive:** Strathfield → Royal National Park → Sea Cliff Bridge → Kiama (~2 hrs + stops)

### [Otford Lookout](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Otford+Lookout+NSW)

![Otford Lookout](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Otford%20Lookout.jpg?width=400)\
[_📷 Google Images_](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Otford+Lookout+NSW)

Before Bald Hill, a 30-second detour off Lawrence Hargrave Drive to this lesser-known elevated viewpoint — quieter than Bald Hill with a framed coastline view looking south toward Sea Cliff Bridge. Perfect warm-up shot.

- **Access:** 1-min walk from the roadside pull-off. No facilities.
- **Crowds:** Almost zero

### [Helensburgh Glow Worm Tunnel](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Helensburgh+Glow+Worm+Tunnel) _(optional, ~20 min)_

![Helensburgh Glow Worm Tunnel](https://img.atlasobscura.com/OyWBId0GUsRGFlek_Dl7dTPsrQxBKZvms1M0shajMVM/rs:fill:780:520:1/g:ce/q:81/sm:1/scp:1/ar:1/aHR0cHM6Ly9hdGxh/cy1kZXYuczMuYW1h/em9uYXdzLmNvbS91/cGxvYWRzL3BsYWNl/X2ltYWdlcy9iYzcz/ZWNhNy1iNzJkLTRj/ODUtYjhiOC1iNjRk/NzE2Y2JlOTFiMTg1/OWRjMTRmZjViNjA3/Mzlfd29ybSB0dW5u/ZWwgMi5qcGc.jpg?width=400)\
[_📷 Google Images_](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Helensburgh+Glow+Worm+Tunnel)

A decommissioned railway tunnel now filled with glow worms. Walk 100m into the dark tunnel, point your torch at the ground, and the ceiling glows blue-green. A unique shot — only worth it if you're passing through and the tunnel isn't flooded after recent rain. Wear shoes you don't mind getting muddy.

- **Access:** Park at the corner of Tunnel Road & Old Station Street; 5-min bush walk
- **Note:** Floods after heavy rain — check conditions. Bring a torch.

---

### [Bald Hill Lookout (Stanwell Tops)](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Bald+Hill+Lookout+Stanwell+Tops)

First photo stop of the trip. Elevated 300m above the Pacific, this windswept headland is a paragliding launch point and gives you a sweeping panorama over the coastline, Sea Cliff Bridge, and the Illawarra escarpment stretching south.

- **Photo style:** Wide panoramic — cliff-edge road snaking below, ocean to the left, green escarpment to the right
- **Best time:** Morning for side-light on the cliff face
- **Access:** 2-min walk from car park, sealed path
- **Parking:** Free, at the lookout

### [Sea Cliff Bridge (Clifton / Coalcliff)](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Sea+Cliff+Bridge+NSW)

The icon of the Grand Pacific Drive. This 665-metre balanced cantilever bridge juts out over the ocean, suspended above the sea against a vertical cliff. Walk it on the pedestrian path — standing mid-bridge with the curve disappearing into the rock face is the classic shot.

![Sea Cliff Bridge from above](<https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Flight%20Over%20Sea%20Cliff%20Bridge%20(Unsplash).jpg?width=400>)\
[_📷 Google Images_](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Sea+Cliff+Bridge+NSW)

- **Photo style:** Leading lines (bridge curves into cliffs), drone shot from Bald Hill above, close-up of waves hitting the columns below
- **Best time:** Morning (sun illuminates the cliff face from the east)
- **Access:** Park at northern or southern end; pedestrian walkway on the ocean side. ~20 min walk end-to-end
- **Parking:** Small lots at both ends of the bridge (fills early on weekends)

![Sea Cliff Bridge coastline view](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Sea%20Cliff%20Bridge,%20Wollongong,%20NSW.jpg?width=400)

> **Tip:** The best wide-angle shot is from the lookout platform mid-bridge. For an alternative frame, walk the bush track above the bridge (accessed from the southern car park) — you'll get a top-down view.

**Drive Sea Cliff Bridge → Thirroul: ~15 min** — continue south on Lawrence Hargrave Drive.

### [Thirroul Beach](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Thirroul+Beach+NSW)

![Thirroul Beach](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Thirroul%20Beach.jpg?width=400)\
[_📷 Google Images_](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Thirroul+Beach+NSW)

Thirroul is a laid-back beach village wedged between the escarpment and the ocean — known for its artsy vibe, heritage shops, and the long, clean sweep of Thirroul Beach. The beach itself has a classic swimming-pool-on-the-rocks at the southern end (Thirroul Baths) and the escarpment rises dramatically behind the town. Grab a coffee at one of the cafes on the main strip, shoot the beach + pool + escarpment composition, and move on.

- **Best time:** Morning — sun illuminates the escarpment behind the town
- **Access:** Park on Lawrence Hargrave Drive or at the beach car park; 2-min walk
- **Parking:** Free street parking
- **Crowds:** Local surfers and swimmers — not touristy

---

**Drive Thirroul → Minnamurra: ~15 min** — through Wollongong, then join the M1/Princes Highway.

---

### [Minnamurra River Mouth & Mystics Beach](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Mystics+Beach+Minnamurra+NSW)

![Minnamurra Mystics Beach](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Minnamurra_River_w-sandbank.jpg?width=400)\
[_📷 Google Images_](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Mystics+Beach+Minnamurra+NSW)

A local secret. Minnamurra is a sleepy riverside village where the Minnamurra River meets the ocean, creating a wide, calm estuary framed by Stack Island (a grassy volcanic plug) on one side and Mystics Beach on the other. Virtually zero tourists — it's where Shellharbour locals go to escape Kiama crowds.

**Mystics Beach:** Walk through a short patch of coastal rainforest (5 min from the car) and you emerge onto a wild, often-empty stretch of golden sand. The southern end of the beach has rock platforms and small caves accessible at low tide.

**Minnamurra River Mouth:** Calm lagoon-like waters, paddleboarders, families. Stack Island sits offshore like a green pyramid — beautiful at golden hour.

- **Photo style:** Stack Island reflection in the calm river at golden hour, rainforest-fringed empty beach, drone shots of the river-meets-ocean curve
- **Best time:** Morning — the light hits Stack Island from over the ocean
- **Access:** Park at the Minnamurra River Reserve or Mystics car park at the end of Charles Avenue. Short walks
- **Parking:** Free, usually plenty of spots
- **Crowds:** Very few — this is not on the tourist radar

---

**Drive Minnamurra → Kiama: ~10 min** — back on the highway, 1 exit south.

### [Kiama Blowhole & Lighthouse](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Kiama+Blowhole+NSW)

Kiama's big blowhole is the world's largest, blasting seawater up to 30 metres high through a natural rock funnel when the swell is right. Right next to it: the 1887 Kiama Lighthouse with its candy-stripe white-and-red tower — the classic coastal contrast shot.

![Kiama Blowhole in action](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Kiama%20Blowhole%20in%20Action.jpg?width=400)\
[_📷 Google Images_](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Kiama+Blowhole+NSW)

- **Photo style:** Spray plume against the lighthouse, slow-shutter dreamy water around the basalt rock platform, sunset silhouette of the lighthouse
- **Best time:** Mid-afternoon to sunset (the blowhole performs best ~2 hrs before high tide with an E–SE swell)
- **Access:** 2-min walk from the Blowhole Point car park; sealed path, fully accessible
- **Parking:** Large car park at Blowhole Point (free)

![Kiama Lighthouse & Blowhole Point](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Kiama%20Blowhole%20and%20Kiama%20Light,%20postcard.JPG?width=400)\
[_📷 Google Images_](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Kiama+Lighthouse)

> **Check tide times before you go:** The blowhole is most dramatic on a rising tide with a swell from the east or southeast. During calm flat seas, it barely sprays. Locals also recommend the **Little Blowhole** (at Tingira Crescent) — less famous, often more impressive, and usually deserted.

---

### [Black Beach & Kiama Harbour](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Black+Beach+Kiama+Harbour)

Just 2 min from the blowhole, on the sheltered western side of Blowhole Point. Black Beach is a dark-sand cove named for its charcoal-coloured grains — it's completely different from the golden beaches down the coast. The sand gets its colour from the basalt geology of the area and creates beautiful, moody contrast against the turquoise water and green headlands.

Next to it, **Kiama Harbour** is a working fishing harbour with colourful trawlers, timber jetties, and still water that reflects the surrounding hills like a mirror at golden hour. Zero crowds — tourists walk from the blowhole car park to the lighthouse and drive away without ever seeing this side.

- **Photo style:** Dark sand contrast against bright water, fishing boats reflected in calm harbour water at sunset, the timber harbour jetty leading into the frame, pilot boat station with its red roof against green hills
- **Best time:** Mid-afternoon for the harbour reflections, sunset for the beach
- **Access:** Walk ~2 min from Blowhole Point (past the swimming pool) or park at Black Beach car park (Shoalhaven Street)
- **Parking:** Small car park at Black Beach, or walk from Blowhole Point
- **Crowds:** None — locals only

> **Also worth seeing:** **Kendalls Beach**, a 5-min drive south — a beautiful sweeping bay with the Kiama headland framing one end. Popular with locals walking dogs but rarely with tourists. Good for a mid-afternoon beach shot with the green hills rolling into the sea.

---

### [Cathedral Rocks at Jones Beach](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Cathedral+Rocks+Kiama+NSW)

A 5-min drive south of Kiama town centre, tucked away from the main tourist trail. Two massive basalt rock pillars rise from the ocean just offshore, with waves thundering through the gap between them. At low tide, you can walk onto an extensive rock platform for foreground interest — barnacles, rock pools teeming with sea life, and fern-like seaweed. The pillars catch the last light of the day from the side, glowing orange against a pink sky.

Jones Beach itself is long, quiet, and backed by dunes — almost always empty. The combination of the rock formation, the empty beach, and the clean horizon makes this one of the best photography stops on the coast. It's used for wedding shoots for a reason.

- **Photo style:** Long-exposure waves wrapping around the pillars, golden-hour side-light on the basalt columns, reflections in the tidal pools, the dune grass and empty sweep of Jones Beach as a wider landscape
- **Best time:** Golden hour / sunset — the light hits the pillars from the side and the sky turns pink behind them
- **Access:** 5-min walk from the Jones Beach car park down to the rock platform (**check tides — accessible at low tide only**)
- **Parking:** Small gravel car park at the north end of Jones Beach (off North Kiama Drive)
- **Crowds:** Low — mostly surfers and dog walkers. Weekday evenings: often completely empty

---

### [Bombo Quarry (sunset shoot)](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Bombo+Quarry+Kiama+NSW)

A decommissioned basalt quarry on Bombo Headland that looks like another planet. Hexagonal basalt columns (like Giant's Causeway), sheer cliff faces carved by the quarrying, and a dramatic sea wall. The contrast between the industrial past and the wild ocean makes it a photographer's playground.

![Bombo Quarry — basalt columns and sea wall](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Bombo%20Quarry.jpg?width=400)\
[_📷 Google Images_](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Bombo+Quarry+Kiama+NSW)

- **Photo style:** Moody long-exposures of waves surging through the columnar basalt drain, portraits framed by the basalt pillars, sunset on the Pacific beyond the quarry wall
- **Best time:** Sunset — the sun sets directly over the ocean from the headland here. Golden light on the basalt columns is stunning
- **Access:** Park at the Bombo Headland car park (end of Darien Ave), then a 15-min walk along the track to the quarry. Uneven ground, rocks, and waves — wear sturdy shoes and watch your step
- **Parking:** Small gravel lot at Darien Avenue, or street parking nearby

> **Safety note:** The quarry has unfenced cliff edges and rogue waves can sweep the platform. Keep an eye on the tide and stay away from wet, dark rock near the water.

---

### Evening — Kiama town

After sunset, drive 5 min back into Kiama. Grab dinner on Terralong Street — **Zia's Kitchen** (modern Italian, BYO) or **The Hungry Monkey** (burgers + craft beer, casual). From the harbour, you can shoot the lighthouse at night — it's lit after dark and reflects beautifully in the harbour water.

## Day 2 — Saturday

> **Drive:** Kiama → Werri Beach → Hyams Beach / Booderee NP → Sydney (~3.5 hrs driving + stops)

### [Werri Beach & Gerringong](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Werri+Beach+Gerringong+NSW)

![Werri Beach Gerringong](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Gerringong_and_Werri_Beach.jpg?width=400)\
[_📷 Google Images_](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Werri+Beach+Gerringong+NSW)

20 min south of Kiama on the coast road. Werri Beach is a 2 km crescent of golden sand wrapped around green rolling hills — the classic South Coast postcard. You'll know it from every Australian tourism brochure: cows on the headland, surfers in the water, the Gerringong headland framing the northern end.

- **Photo style:** Hills-meet-ocean landscapes, drone panorama of the crescent bay, long-lens compression of the headland
- **Best time:** Morning — the sun rises behind you over the hills, illuminating the beach and headland
- **Access:** Park at the south or north end of Werri Beach; walk along the sand or the coastal path
- **Parking:** Free street parking along Werri Street

> **Quick detour:** Drive up to the **Gerringong Boat Harbour** for a different angle looking down on the beach from the headland.

---

### [Seven Mile Beach (Gerroa)](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Seven+Mile+Beach+NSW)

A 5-min drive from Gerringong. As the name suggests: seven miles of uninterrupted sand stretching south towards Shoalhaven Heads. This is a great spot to just pull over, walk, and shoot — nobody around.

- **Photo style:** Minimalist seascapes — empty sand, rolling dunes, lone figures
- **Best time:** Mid-morning for clear blue water
- **Access:** Multiple pull-offs along Crooked River Road

---

### [Callala Beach (Jervis Bay)](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Callala+Beach+Jervis+Bay)

![Callala Beach](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/CallalaBeach-Huskisson-PC220151.JPG?width=400)\
[_📷 Google Images_](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Callala+Beach+Jervis+Bay)

Before reaching Huskisson, take a 10-min detour via Myola Road to the northern shore of Jervis Bay. Callala Beach is a 5 km sweep of flour-soft white sand on the bay's quieter northern side — it looks like Hyams Beach but with about 90% fewer people. The water here is just as turquoise, and because it faces south across the bay, the light is softer and the reflections cleaner.

At the northern tip, the beach curls around a sand spit into Currambene Creek — the kind of natural geometry that looks incredible from a drone. Kangaroos graze on the grass behind the dunes, and the bay is so sheltered the water barely ripples.

- **Photo style:** Empty white sand + turquoise water without the Hyams crowds, sand-spit curves at Currambene Creek, kangaroos silhouetted against the bay at dusk
- **Best time:** Mid-to-late morning — high sun makes the water glow, south-facing = no harsh direct sun
- **Access:** Park at Callala Beach car park (end of Quay Road); 2-min walk to the beach
- **Parking:** Free, rarely full even on weekends
- **Crowds:** Very low — locals know about it, tourists don't

---

### [Huskisson (lunch break)](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Huskisson+Jervis+Bay+NSW)

~25 min from Callala Beach via Woollamia Road. Huskisson is the buzzing little hub of Jervis Bay with cafes, a waterfront pub, and dolphin/whale-watching tours departing from the wharf. Grab lunch at **The Salty Crab** (fish & chips on the wharf) or **5 Little Pigs** (best cafe in town). If you have an hour to spare, a 2-hr dolphin cruise is well worth it — Jervis Bay has a resident pod of ~80 bottlenose dolphins.

---

### [Hyams Beach (Jervis Bay)](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Hyams+Beach+Jervis+Bay+NSW)

![Hyams Beach](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Hyams_Beach._Booderee_National_Park_%26_Jervis_Bay_Marine_Park.jpg?width=400)\
[_📷 Google Images_](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Hyams+Beach+Jervis+Bay+NSW)

The famous white-sand beach. The sand here is so white and fine it squeaks underfoot, and the water is the colour of a Fiji lagoon. Very few other beaches in NSW look like this. Only 10 min from Huskisson.

- **Photo style:** Turquoise water + white sand contrast, aerial/overhead shots of the sand ripples, the jetty and the rocks at the southern end for foreground interest
- **Best time:** Midday — the high sun makes the water glow Caribbean-turquoise. Also good at sunrise if you want it empty
- **Access:** Park at Hyams Beach village car park (fills very early on weekends — aim to arrive before 10am or later in the afternoon). 2-min walk to the beach
- **Parking:** Village car park (limited); if full, park at the top of Cyrus Street and walk down

> **Note:** Hyams Beach is one-way-in, one-way-out and the council closes access once the village's 450 spots fill up. Getting there before 10am is strongly advised. No cafes here — bring water.

---

### [Booderee National Park (Cave Beach, Murrays Beach & Green Patch)](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Murrays+Beach+Booderee+Jervis+Bay)

Booderee is an Aboriginal-owned national park at the southern end of Jervis Bay. Entry fee is ~$13 per vehicle (national park pass required). Three of the bay's best beaches are inside — we'll hit two.

**Murrays Beach (must-visit, very quiet):** The best-kept secret in Booderee. A crescent of white sand in a sheltered cove with water so clear you can see the bottom at 10 metres. Calm, shallow, and ringed by bushland — no waves, no wind, just mirror-flat water. There's a rocky island 100 metres offshore (Bowen Island) that adds a perfect focal point to any composition. Usually only a handful of people here, even on a Saturday. If you only do one beach in Booderee, make it this one.

**Cave Beach:** A 300m walk down a bush track opens onto white sand framed by cliffs. Sea caves at the southern end accessible at low tide. Popular with surfers, but the caves and rock formations at the far end are usually empty.

**Green Patch (optional):** A sheltered cove with BBQs under the trees and kangaroos grazing on the grass right behind the beach. Good if you want a nature + wildlife combo, but busier than Murrays.

- **Photo style:** Murrays — mirror-calm turquoise water with Bowen Island in frame; Cave — sea caves with light spilling through; Green Patch — kangaroos + beach
- **Best time:** Late afternoon — soft golden light, kangaroos active
- **Access:** Sealed roads; 2-min walk to Murrays, 5-min walk to Cave
- **Parking:** Designated car parks at each (free with park entry)
- **Crowds:** Murrays — almost empty. Cave — surfers at the main break, empty at the southern end. Green Patch — moderate

---

### [Point Perpendicular Lighthouse](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Point+Perpendicular+Lighthouse+Jervis+Bay)

![Point Perpendicular](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Point_Perpendicular_Lighthouse.jpg?width=400)\
[_📷 Google Images_](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Point+Perpendicular+Lighthouse+Jervis+Bay)

At the very northern tip of Jervis Bay, accessed via a short detour through Beecroft Peninsula (part of the Beecroft Weapons Range — check if open to the public on your dates). This dramatic lighthouse sits atop 90-metre cliffs, looking straight down into the deep blue. Whales breach below during migration season.

- **Photo style:** Vertigo-inducing cliff shots, lighthouse against endless ocean, whale spouts dotting the water below (binoculars or long lens)
- **Best time:** Late afternoon for side-light on the cliffs
- **Access:** ~30 min drive from Hyams Beach via unsealed road; 5-min walk from car park to the lighthouse
- **Note:** The weapons range sometimes closes the road on weekdays. Check the schedule at the Shoalhaven council website before going. If closed, skip to the return drive.

---

### Stop 8 (optional) — [Honeymoon Bay](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Honeymoon+Bay+Jervis+Bay+NSW)

A tiny hidden beach tucked inside the Beecroft Peninsula, just before Point Perpendicular. Crystal-clear water, sheltered from swell, surrounded by bushland. If you have the light and the energy after a long day, this is the cherry on top. The snorkelling is excellent if you brought gear.

---

### Return drive — Jervis Bay → Strathfield (~3 hrs)

Take the Princes Highway (A1) north via Nowra and then up the Hume Motorway (M31) to Sydney. This is the fastest route back (~2.5–3 hrs, depending on traffic). Aim to leave Jervis Bay by 5pm to arrive back at Strathfield by 8pm.

> Stop for a final coffee at **Berry** — a charming heritage town 15 min north of Nowra with excellent cafes (The Berry Tea Shop, Milkwood Bakery) and a famous donut van (Berry Donut Van — if it's open).

> **Jervis Bay bioluminescence (seasonal):** On warm nights, the waters of Jervis Bay sometimes light up with bioluminescent plankton — glowing neon-blue in the breaking waves. It peaks in late summer/autumn (Feb–May) but has been spotted as early as September. If you're still in the bay after dark, check the water at Plantation Point (Vincentia) or Barfleur Beach — that's where it's most commonly reported. If conditions are against it, Jervis Bay is also a designated dark-sky area — excellent milky way conditions on a clear night. Worth staying for the stars even if the bioluminescence doesn't show up.

## Accommodation

_Overnight: Kiama or nearby (Friday night only). Book ahead — weekends fill up fast in the South Coast._

Look for accommodation with **private bathroom, free parking, and WiFi** near Kiama town centre. Shortlist to check on Booking.com:

- **Nova Kiama** — modern rooms, walk to blowhole, free parking, couples-friendly
- **Kiama Shores** — central, opposite the harbour, free parking
- **Big4 Easts Beach Holiday Park** — cabins at Easts Beach, coastal bush setting, budget option
- Airbnbs in Kiama Heights or Bombo — many clifftop cottages with ocean views

> Book something within walking distance of the blowhole/lighthouse for a quick twilight re-shoot before dinner.

## Practical Notes

- **Whale season:** September is peak southern right + humpback whale migration. Take binoculars or a long lens — the headlands at Kiama and Point Perpendicular are excellent whale-watching spots. You'll often see spouts and breaches without even trying.
- **Tides:** Check the tide chart before leaving. Kiama Blowhole < high tide; Cathedral Rocks rock platform < low tide; Cave Beach sea caves < low tide. Plan the route around the tide.
- **Fuel:** Fill up at Wollongong or Kiama. Fuel gets sparse south of Nowra.
- **What to bring:** Sturdy shoes for Bombo Quarry and rock platforms, warm layers (coastal wind in September), sunscreen, water, tripod for long exposures
- **Phone signal:** Good throughout most of the route. Patchy in Booderee NP and Beecroft Peninsula — download offline maps before leaving
- **Park pass:** Booderee National Park requires a Parks Australia pass (~$13 per vehicle / 48 hrs). Buy at the park entry gate or [online](https://parksaustralia.gov.au/booderee/)

---

> **Final note:** This is a photographer's road trip — 15+ photo stops in 2 days, carefully balanced between iconic coastal landmarks (Sea Cliff Bridge, Kiama Blowhole, Hyams Beach) and crowd-free hidden gems (Mystics Beach, Black Beach, Callala Beach, Murrays Beach). The light in September is clean and crisp, the whale migration adds wild drama, and we're driving one of the best coastal routes in NSW. Pack your tripod, charge everything, and go.
