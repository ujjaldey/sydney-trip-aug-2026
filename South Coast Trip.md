Grand Pacific Drive is one of Australia's most scenic coastal routes — 140 km of cliff-hugging roads from Royal National Park down to Nowra. In September (early spring), the coast is green after winter rains, skies are clear, and the southern right whale migration overlaps with humpback season. We're focusing on **less-crowded natural beaches** — skipping the tourist traps and going where locals go.

### Sun & Tide Planning

**Sun — Fri 4 Sep 2026 (Kiama):**

|                     | Time          |
| ------------------- | ------------- |
| Sunrise             | 6:12 am       |
| Sunset              | 5:39 pm       |
| Evening golden hour | ~4:50–5:40 pm |
| Blue hour           | ~5:40–6:05 pm |

**Tides — Kiama Harbour (approx.):**

|             | Time     |
| ----------- | -------- |
| First high  | 9:26 am  |
| Second low  | 3:23 pm  |
| Second high | ~9:45 pm |

> 🌗 **Neap-tide week.** The moon is near first-quarter (lunar eclipse was 27–28 Aug), so the range is small (~0.5–0.6 m) — highs won't be huge, but the exposed rock is still reachable at low tide.

**How this shapes the day (read this before anything else):**

1. **First high (9:26 am)** happens while you're at the northern end — Kellys Falls, Bald Hill, Sea Cliff Bridge and Hill 60 are all light-driven stops, so the high tide doesn't matter there.
2. **Falling tide all afternoon** → **Bushrangers Bay rock pools** are at their best **~1:10–2:15 pm** (approaching the 3:23 pm low). This is your **rock-pool priority stop** — give it time, not a fly-by.
3. **Second low (3:23 pm)** → **Cathedral Rocks** platform is fully exposed from ~3:40 pm. Shoot the platform, then the golden hour (~4:50 pm) as the water slowly floods back. **The money shot of the day.**
4. **Big Kiama Blowhole** only performs near a high tide. With an 8 am start the 9:26 am window is unreachable → **deferred to Day 2 morning** (Sat high ~10:15 am; best ~8:15 am). Kept in the plan + map.
5. **Little Blowhole** fires on much smaller swells than the big one → **quick blue-hour bonus stop (~6 pm)** on the rising tide after Bombo.
6. **Bombo Quarry sunset** — the sun sets over the ocean at 5:39 pm; best light 5:00–5:45 pm. Cathedral Rocks and Bombo are only ~5 min apart.
7. **Killalea** (optional) also lands near the 3:23 pm low, so its southern rock pools are exposed ~2:25–2:55 pm if you want them.

### [Kellys Falls Track](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Kellys+Falls+NSW)

<img src="https://encrypted-tbn0.gstatic.com/images?q=tbn:ANd9GcQ74vgdk5Byn0pepZ3unsbrmrFHanWlXIvLZDoDzuuTBxTDWmZqrAqw4aJP&s=10" width="400" alt="Kellys Falls picnic area — Garawarra State Conservation Area">\
[_📷 Google Images_](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Kellys+Falls+NSW)

A short detour off the F6/Princes corridor (Helensburgh exit) before you join the Grand Pacific Drive proper. A cascading waterfall drops over layered sandstone into a plunge pool in a rainforest-lined gully — in the **Garawarra State Conservation Area** (right next to Royal National Park, but **free to enter**, no park fee). Four lookout platforms peer down into the gully, and a short, steep set of steps drops down closer to the falls.

- **Photo style:** Long-exposure cascades + plunge pool (bring a polariser/ND filter), fern-lined gully scenes, top-down lookouts
- **Best time:** Morning — soft light in the gully, few people. Flow is at its best a few days after rain; in dry spells the cascades thin out
- **Access:** Park at the Kellys Falls picnic area car park (off Kellys Falls Road); 2–3 min walk to the nearest lookout, steeper steps down to the falls
- **Parking:** Free bitumen car park at the picnic area
- **Note:** Rocks and steps are extremely slippery when wet; short unsealed section of road to the car park (2WD-fine)
- **Verdict:** ⚡ **Go if time** — lovely morning start; skip only if the drive has put you behind

First photo stop of the trip. Elevated 300m above the Pacific, this windswept headland is a paragliding launch point and gives you a sweeping panorama over the coastline, Sea Cliff Bridge, and the Illawarra escarpment stretching south.

- **Photo style:** Wide panoramic — cliff-edge road snaking below, ocean to the left, green escarpment to the right
- **Best time:** Morning for side-light on the cliff face (sunrise 6:12 am is just before you arrive — ideal first-light mood)
- **Access:** 2-min walk from car park, sealed path
- **Parking:** Free, at the lookout
- **Verdict:** ⚡ **Go if time** — a 5-min pullover that's nearly free; don't linger

### [Sea Cliff Bridge (Clifton / Coalcliff)](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Sea+Cliff+Bridge+NSW)

The icon of the Grand Pacific Drive. This 665-metre balanced cantilever bridge juts out over the ocean, suspended above the sea against a vertical cliff. Walk it on the pedestrian path — standing mid-bridge with the curve disappearing into the rock face is the classic shot.

![Sea Cliff Bridge from above](<https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Flight%20Over%20Sea%20Cliff%20Bridge%20(Unsplash).jpg?width=400>)\
[_📷 Google Images_](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Sea+Cliff+Bridge+NSW)

- **Photo style:** Leading lines (bridge curves into cliffs), drone shot from Bald Hill above, close-up of waves hitting the columns below
- **Best time:** Morning (sun illuminates the cliff face from the east)
- **Access:** Park at northern or southern end; pedestrian walkway on the ocean side. ~20 min walk end-to-end
- **Parking:** Small lots at both ends of the bridge (fills early on weekends)

![Sea Cliff Bridge coastline view](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Sea%20Cliff%20Bridge,%20Wollongong,%20NSW.jpg?width=400)

> **Tip:** The best wide-angle shot is from the lookout platform mid-bridge. For an alternative frame, walk the bush track above the bridge (accessed from the southern car park) — you'll get a top-down view.

- **Verdict:** ✅ **Must-go** — the icon of the drive; morning light on the eastern cliffs is unrepeatable

### [Thirroul Beach](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Thirroul+Beach+NSW) _(optional)_

![Thirroul Beach](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Thirroul%20Beach.jpg?width=400)\
[_📷 Google Images_](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Thirroul+Beach+NSW)

Thirroul is a laid-back beach village wedged between the escarpment and the ocean — known for its artsy vibe, heritage shops, and the long, clean sweep of Thirroul Beach. The beach itself has a classic swimming-pool-on-the-rocks at the southern end (Thirroul Baths) and the escarpment rises dramatically behind the town. Grab a coffee at one of the cafes on the main strip, shoot the beach + pool + escarpment composition, and move on.

- **Best time:** Morning — sun illuminates the escarpment behind the town
- **Access:** Park on Lawrence Hargrave Drive or at the beach car park; 2-min walk
- **Parking:** Free street parking
- **Crowds:** Local surfers and swimmers — not touristy
- **Verdict:** ⏭️ **Optional** — coffee stop only. Skip if you want the buffer for the rock-pool stop.

### [Port Kembla Lookout (Hill 60)](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Hill+60+Lookout+Port+Kembla)

![Hill 60 Lookout, Port Kembla](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Hill%2060%20port%20kembla%20nsw6-7.jpg?width=400)\
[_📷 Google Images_](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Hill+60+Lookout+Port+Kembla)

A 71-metre headland that's equal parts history and panorama. Hill 60 was the site of the **Illowra Battery** — a World War II coastal-artillery fortification — and the concrete gun emplacements still sit in the grass, staring out over the Pacific. From the top you get a sweeping 360° view: the ocean and beaches below (Port Kembla Beach, Fishermans Beach), the industrial port and steelworks to the west, and the Illawarra Escarpment behind. A genuinely unique composition — military relics framing an industrial-meets-coastal vista.

- **Photo style:** Gun emplacements as foreground with ocean behind, coastal panorama, long-lens compression of the port + escarpment
- **Best time:** Morning to early afternoon — the lookout faces east, so the ocean stays lit for most of the day; late afternoon backlights the water
- **Access:** Short, well-formed walking track from the small car park on Military Road; stairs involved
- **Parking:** Free on-site parking
- **Note:** The WW2 tunnels/bunkers are sealed — don't expect to go inside. Not wheelchair-accessible
- **Verdict:** ⚡ **Go if time** — quick, unique stop; skip if you need the buffer for the rock pools

### [Bushrangers Bay (Bass Point)](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Bushrangers+Bay+Bass+Point+NSW)

<img src="https://images.squarespace-cdn.com/content/v1/596b2969d2b85786e6892853/507d9708-42a4-48be-a939-0f3ee1f0f9b0/DJI_0008-Edit-6.jpg" width="400" alt="Bushrangers Bay — Bass Point">\
[_📷 Google Images_](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Bushrangers+Bay+Bass+Point+NSW)

A sheltered, almost-untouched bay tucked into the southern side of Bass Point, just south of Shellharbour. The turquoise rock pools scattered across the platform are the draw — the water glows bright aqua against the dark rock even at midday. The bay itself is an **aquatic reserve** (no fishing, abundant marine life) and the headland is a known whale-watching vantage during migration. No facilities, no crowds — just you, the pools, and the sound of the swell.

- **Photo style:** Turquoise rock pools with long-exposure water, the empty bay from the coastal track, whale spouts on the horizon (binoculars or long lens)
- **Best time:** 🌊 **Low/mid tide — 1:10–2:15 pm.** The tide is falling toward the 3:23 pm low, so the pools are filling with clean water and the platform is opening up. **This is the rock-pool priority stop of Day 1 — allow an hour.**
- **Access:** ~900 m easy return walk from the Bushrangers Bay car park (end of Bass Point Tourist Rd; last ~1.7 km unsealed but 2WD-fine)
- **Parking:** Free car park; open 6:30 am – 8:00 pm (gated), closed on total fire-ban days
- **Safety:** Unpatrolled; stay off wet, dark rock when the swell is up
- **Verdict:** ✅ **Must-go — rock-pool priority.** Give it the full hour

### [Killalea Beach — The Farm](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Killalea+Beach+The+Farm+NSW) _(optional)_

![Killalea State Park](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Killalea%20State%20Park.jpg?width=400)\
[_📷 Google Images_](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Killalea+Beach+The+Farm+NSW)

A **National Surfing Reserve** locals simply call "The Farm" — consistently voted one of Australia's best beaches. A wide, unpatrolled surf beach curls below rolling green headlands backed by remnant rainforest and a lagoon. From the headland lookout near the kiosk you get the classic South Coast frame: emerald grass hills over golden sand and blue water. At the southern end, a scramble over the rocks leads to a hidden swimming pool ("Dream Pool") where waves pour in — a gorgeous long-exposure subject.

- **Photo style:** Green headlands over the beach from the lookout, surf action, long-exposure waves in the hidden southern rock pool
- **Best time:** 🌊 2:25–2:55 pm — tide is at/near the 3:23 pm low, so the southern rock pools and "Dream Pool" are exposed. Midday light keeps the green-to-turquoise contrast strong
- **Access:** Free car park on Killalea Drive; short walk to the beach, lookout platform near The Farm Kiosk
- **Parking:** Free; facilities (toilets, picnic tables) at the picnic area
- **Note:** Unpatrolled surf beach with strong rips — swim with care. No pets. "The Farm" kiosk is run by the local Aboriginal community
- **Verdict:** ⏭️ **Optional** — keep only if you're on schedule; the rock pools are the bonus here after Bushrangers Bay.

### [Minnamurra River Mouth & Mystics Beach](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Mystics+Beach+Minnamurra+NSW) _(optional)_

![Minnamurra Mystics Beach](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Minnamurra_River_w-sandbank.jpg?width=400)\
[_📷 Google Images_](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Mystics+Beach+Minnamurra+NSW)

A local secret. Minnamurra is a sleepy riverside village where the Minnamurra River meets the ocean, creating a wide, calm estuary framed by Stack Island (a grassy volcanic plug) on one side and Mystics Beach on the other. Virtually zero tourists — it's where Shellharbour locals go to escape Kiama crowds.

**Mystics Beach:** Walk through a short patch of coastal rainforest (5 min from the car) and you emerge onto a wild, often-empty stretch of golden sand. The southern end of the beach has rock platforms and small caves accessible at low tide.

**Minnamurra River Mouth:** Calm lagoon-like waters, paddleboarders, families. Stack Island sits offshore like a green pyramid — beautiful at golden hour.

- **Photo style:** Stack Island reflection in the calm river at golden hour, rainforest-fringed empty beach, drone shots of the river-meets-ocean curve
- **Best time:** Morning is ideal (the light hits Stack Island from over the ocean) — on this route you'll pass it ~3:05 pm, so grab a quick river/beach frame and move on. Golden hour here loses out to Cathedral Rocks + Bombo
- **Access:** Park at the Minnamurra River Reserve or Mystics car park at the end of Charles Avenue. Short walks
- **Parking:** Free, usually plenty of spots
- **Crowds:** Very few — this is not on the tourist radar
- **Verdict:** ⏭️ **Optional** — quick 15-min look only; skip if you're behind.

### [Cathedral Rocks at Jones Beach](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Cathedral+Rocks+Kiama+NSW)

<img src="https://images.squarespace-cdn.com/content/v1/596b2969d2b85786e6892853/1740324452019-P7SAVKGKWW6793H0BN8Z/0K3A0197-Edit-2.jpg" width="400" alt="Cathedral Rocks at Jones Beach — Kiama coastline">\
[_📷 Google Images_](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Cathedral+Rocks+Kiama+NSW)

A 5-min drive north of Kiama town centre, tucked away from the main tourist trail. Two massive basalt rock pillars rise from the ocean just offshore, with waves thundering through the gap between them. At low tide, you can walk onto an extensive rock platform for foreground interest — barnacles, rock pools teeming with sea life, and fern-like seaweed. The pillars catch the last light of the day from the side, glowing orange against a pink sky.

Jones Beach itself is long, quiet, and backed by dunes — almost always empty. The combination of the rock formation, the empty beach, and the clean horizon makes this one of the best photography stops on the coast. It's used for wedding shoots for a reason.

- **Photo style:** Long-exposure waves wrapping around the pillars, golden-hour side-light on the basalt columns, reflections in the tidal pools, the dune grass and empty sweep of Jones Beach as a wider landscape
- **Best time:** 🌊 **Low tide 3:23 pm + golden hour 4:50 pm.** Arrive ~3:40 pm while the platform is still fully exposed, shoot the pillars + tidal pools, then stay as the light turns gold and the water floods slowly back. **Allow 1–1.5 hrs — this is the money shot of Day 1.**
- **Access:** 5-min walk from the Jones Beach car park down to the rock platform (**accessible at low tide only**; on this neap tide expect partial exposure later in the session — pick foreground from the rock pools)
- **Parking:** Small gravel car park at the north end of Jones Beach (off North Kiama Drive)
- **Crowds:** Low — mostly surfers and dog walkers. Weekday evenings: often completely empty
- **Verdict:** ✅ **Must-go — top priority of Day 1.** The money shot; don't rush it

### [Bombo Quarry (sunset shoot)](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Bombo+Quarry+Kiama+NSW)

A decommissioned basalt quarry on Bombo Headland that looks like another planet. Hexagonal basalt columns (like Giant's Causeway), sheer cliff faces carved by the quarrying, and a dramatic sea wall. The contrast between the industrial past and the wild ocean makes it a photographer's playground.

![Bombo Quarry — basalt columns and sea wall](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Bombo%20Quarry.jpg?width=400)\
[_📷 Google Images_](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Bombo+Quarry+Kiama+NSW)

- **Photo style:** Moody long-exposures of waves surging through the columnar basalt drain, portraits framed by the basalt pillars, sunset on the Pacific beyond the quarry wall
- **Best time:** Sunset (5:39 pm on Fri 4 Sep) — the sun sets directly over the ocean from the headland here. Golden light on the basalt columns from ~5:00 pm. Only ~5 min from Cathedral Rocks, so hit Cathedral first, then this for the last light
- **Access:** Park at the Bombo Headland car park (end of Darien Ave), then a 15-min walk along the track to the quarry. Uneven ground, rocks, and waves — wear sturdy shoes and watch your step
- **Parking:** Small gravel lot at Darien Avenue, or street parking nearby

> **Safety note:** The quarry has unfenced cliff edges and rogue waves can sweep the platform. Keep an eye on the tide and stay away from wet, dark rock near the water.

- **Verdict:** ✅ **Must-go — sunset.** The best landscape frame of the day

The Little Blowhole is the locals' secret — smaller than the famous big blowhole, but it fires on a fraction of the swell and is almost always deserted. There's a small fenced viewing platform right above the rocks.

- **Photo style:** Spray plumes against the ocean horizon, long-exposure water churning in the blowhole chasm, blue-hour mood with the town lights behind
- **Best time:** 🌊 **Rising tide + E/SE swell.** On Day 1 that lands around **5:50–6:20 pm** (low was 3:23 pm, tide rising toward the ~9:45 pm high) — a perfect quick blue-hour bonus after Bombo

- **Note:** Check the swell direction on the BOM forecast — if it's from the west, skip it and head straight to the hotel.
- **Verdict:** ⚡ **Go if time** — a 20-min blue-hour bonus; skip if the swell is from the west

### [Plantation Point — Sunset & Jervis Bay Stargazing](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Jervis+Bay+Stargazing+Plantation+Point)

![Milky Way over the ocean](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Milky%20Way%20Streaky%20Bay%20%28Unsplash%29.jpg?width=400)\
[_📷 Google Images_](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=Jervis+Bay+Stargazing)

Plantation Point is a low-light headland and boat-ramp reserve at the south end of Vincentia — the classic Jervis Bay sunset + stargazing spot. The bay faces west here, so you watch the sun drop behind the water, then turn around for some of the darkest skies on the NSW coast (Jervis Bay is a designated dark-sky area).

- **Photo style:** Sunset over Jervis Bay from the headland, silhouetted boat ramp/rocks, Milky Way core rising in the south, bioluminescent glow in the shallows (seasonal)
- **Best time:** Golden hour **4:50–5:40 pm** for sunset; fully dark from **~6:40 pm**. Moon is near last-quarter (rises after midnight), so the evening is moon-free — prime stargazing
- **Jervis Bay Stargazing tour (book ahead):** a 90-min guided session with telescope + astronomer, starting ~6:10 pm (30 min after sunset). Meeting beach is emailed the day before; runs Fri–Sun. Book on [jervisbaystargazing.com.au](https://www.jervisbaystargazing.com.au) — price on booking
- **DIY option:** free — stay at Plantation Point, let your eyes adapt, and scan for bioluminescence in the shallows (most common Sept–Oct on still, dark nights)
- **Access:** Free parking at the boat-ramp car park; level ground
- **Verdict:** ✅ **Must-go — the day's finale.** Plan to leave ~8:30 pm.

## Practical Notes

- **Whale season:** September is peak southern right + humpback whale migration. Take binoculars or a long lens — the headlands at Kiama, Bass Point and Point Perpendicular are excellent whale-watching spots. You'll often see spouts and breaches without even trying.
- **Tides:** Kiama Harbour on Fri 4 Sep — first high 9:26 am, second low 3:23 pm (neap week). Bushrangers Bay & Killalea rock pools ≈ low/mid tide; Cathedral Rocks platform ≈ low tide (3:23 pm); Little Blowhole ≈ rising tide + E/SE swell; Kiama Blowhole ≈ high tide (deferred to Sat, high ~10:15 am); Cave Beach sea caves ≈ low tide (Day 2). Plan the afternoon around the falling tide.
- **Fuel:** Fill up at Wollongong or Kiama. Fuel gets sparse south of Nowra.
- **What to bring:** Sturdy shoes for Bombo Quarry and rock platforms, warm layers (coastal wind in September), sunscreen, water, tripod for long exposures, polariser + ND filter for the pools and waterfall
- **Phone signal:** Good throughout most of the route. Patchy in Booderee NP and Beecroft Peninsula — download offline maps before leaving
- **Park pass:** Booderee National Park requires a Parks Australia pass (~$13 per vehicle / 48 hrs). Buy at the park entry gate or [online](https://parksaustralia.gov.au/booderee/)

> **Final note:** This is a photography-focused road trip — 23 stops (11 on Day 1, 12 on Day 2) anchored around the best light and tide of each day. **Day 1** (8 am start) is tide-driven: **Bushrangers Bay rock pools** (1:10–2:15 pm, falling tide) → **Cathedral Rocks** (3:40–5:00 pm, low tide + golden hour) → **Bombo sunset** (5:39 pm) → **Little Blowhole** (blue hour). **Day 2** heads for Jervis Bay: **Shoalhaven Heads** (morning) → **Honeymoon Bay + Point Perpendicular** (midday) → **Huskisson lunch** → **Hyams Beach** (afternoon) → **Plantation Point sunset + stargazing** (moon-free evening, late return ~11 pm). Thirroul, Killalea, Minnamurra and the old Day 2 stops are kept as optional trims. September gives clean winter light, migrating whales, and quiet beaches. Check the tide, book the stargazing tour ahead, charge everything, and enjoy one of the best coastal drives in NSW.
